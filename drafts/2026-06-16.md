**Why:** FDA classification of SARS-CoV-2 point-of-care device as Class II with special controls

**Summary:** FDA has formally classified simple point-of-care SARS-CoV-2 detection devices as Class II (special controls). The agency says this classification provides reasonable assurance of safety and effectiveness while reducing regulatory burden and improving patient access to these near-patient testing devices.

**Draft:**

FDA just formally classified simple point-of-care SARS-CoV-2 detection devices as Class II (special controls). Not a surprise, exactly, but it's the kind of regulatory housekeeping that actually matters if you work in this space. Class II with special controls is the middle path: more oversight than Class I, but a lighter lift than PMA. The framing in the Federal Register notice is that it both ensures safety and reduces regulatory burden. (Those two things can coexist, though it doesn't always feel that way when you're in the middle of a submission.) Anyway, it's now codified, which is the part that makes it real.

## 7. FDA Designates Zepto Life Technologies Invasive Mold Blood Test Breakthrough Device

**Why:** FDA Breakthrough Device designation for invasive mold blood test IVD

**Summary:** The FDA granted Breakthrough Device designation to Zepto Life Technologies for a blood test for invasive mold infections. This designation is meant to speed up development and review of diagnostics that address serious conditions with unmet needs.

**Draft:**

FDA just gave Breakthrough Device designation to Zepto Life Technologies for a blood test that detects invasive mold infections from blood.

Invasive mold is one of those things that's genuinely hard to catch early, and the current diagnostic options aren't great (lots of imaging, bronchoscopy, empiric treatment). A blood-based test would be a different path.

Breakthrough designation doesn't mean it's cleared, just that FDA will work more closely with them through development and review. Still early. But it's the kind of thing I keep a tab open on when I see it.

## 8. Cepheid Gets CE-IVDR Marking for Multiplex Gastrointestinal Panel

**Why:** CE-IVDR regulatory marking for multiplex molecular diagnostic panel

**Summary:** Cepheid has received CE-IVDR marking for a multiplex gastrointestinal panel. CE-IVDR is the newer, stricter EU in vitro diagnostic regulation that replaced the older CE-IVD marking. Getting this designation means the test has cleared a more rigorous conformity assessment process.

**Draft:**

Cepheid got CE-IVDR marking for their multiplex GI panel. Worth noting if you work in IVD in Europe, because CE-IVDR (as opposed to the older CE-IVD) is the stricter standard that's been rolling in under the EU's updated IVD regulation. Getting there with a multiplex panel isn't a small lift on the regulatory side. I mostly track this stuff because it shows up in the data infrastructure questions eventually, like what metadata travels with a result once the regulatory goalposts have moved.
