# IVD / diagnostics drafts — 2026-09-14

## 1. Medical Devices; Clinical Chemistry and Clinical Toxicology Devices; Classification of the Anti-Tumor Necrosis Factor Alpha Monoclonal Antibody Test System for Inflammatory Bowel Disease

**Why:** FDA classifies new IVD test system (anti-TNF alpha antibody) as Class II with special controls; directly relevant to diagnostic device regulation

**Summary:** FDA has classified the anti-tumor necrosis factor alpha monoclonal antibody test system for inflammatory bowel disease as class II (special controls). The classification is intended to provide reasonable assurance of safety and effectiveness while reducing regulatory burden and improving patient access to the device.

**Draft:**

FDA just classified anti-TNF alpha monoclonal antibody test systems for IBD into class II (special controls). That's the regulatory bucket that comes with defined requirements but doesn't require premarket approval the way class III does.

For people working in IVD, this is the kind of rule that quietly matters. Special controls classifications shape what you have to demonstrate and document before a test gets to a clinician. For therapeutic drug monitoring in IBD, having a clearer regulatory path is useful.

Not a dramatic announcement, just a codified classification. But I find myself reading these things so I know what the framework actually is, not just what people say it is.
