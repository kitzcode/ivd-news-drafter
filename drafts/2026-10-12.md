# IVD / diagnostics drafts — 2026-10-12

## 1. Clinical Laboratory Improvement Amendments of 1988 (CLIA); Virtual Access, Gynecologic Cytology Proficiency Testing (PT), Personnel Qualifications, and Other Changes

**Why:** Direct CLIA regulatory update on virtual access, cytology PT, and personnel qualifications—core compliance topic

**Summary:** CMS has proposed updates to CLIA regulations that would allow virtual access under a primary lab's certificate (except for cytology), permit digital images for gynecologic cytology proficiency testing, and make changes to personnel qualifications and several reporting requirements. The rule is aimed at modernizing oversight while addressing workforce shortages.

**Draft:**

CMS dropped a proposed CLIA rule that caught my eye this week. The big-ish thing: virtual access would be allowed under the primary testing lab's certificate (cytology is carved out, which makes sense given the proficiency testing nuances). Digital images for gynecologic cytology PT is also in there, which has been a long time coming given how the rest of lab medicine has moved.

Personnel qualification changes too, plus various reporting and certificate updates. The workforce shortage framing is woven through the whole thing, which is at least honest about why some of this is moving now.

I'll be reading the personnel section closely. That's usually where the details get interesting (or complicated).

## 2. Medical Devices; Immunology and Microbiology Devices; Classification of the High Throughput DNA Sequencing for Hereditary Cancer Predisposition Assessment Test System

**Why:** FDA classification of NGS-based hereditary cancer test as Class II device—key regulatory action for genetic diagnostics

**Summary:** FDA is classifying high throughput DNA sequencing systems used for hereditary cancer predisposition assessment as Class II devices, subject to special controls. The agency says this classification provides reasonable assurance of safety and effectiveness while reducing regulatory burden and improving patient access.

**Draft:**

FDA just classified high throughput DNA sequencing for hereditary cancer predisposition assessment as Class II (special controls). From a regulatory standpoint, that's a meaningful landing spot for this kind of test. Class II with special controls means the agency thinks the risk is manageable with defined guardrails, rather than requiring the full premarket approval process.

I find these classification decisions interesting to follow. The special controls are where the real detail lives, and I'll be reading through what they actually require. (That part is usually where the practical implications for test developers show up.)

**Source:** Federal Register — https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/06/2026-20443/medical-devices-immunology-and-microbiology-devices-classification-of-the-high-throughput-dna
