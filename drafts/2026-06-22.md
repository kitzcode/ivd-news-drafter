# IVD / diagnostics drafts — 2026-06-22

## 1. Medical Devices; Immunology and Microbiology Devices; Classification of the Simple In Vitro Diagnostic Device for the Detection of Secreted Proteins From Bacillus Species (spp.) in Human Clinical Samples

**Why:** FDA IVD device classification order for Bacillus protein detection—direct regulatory action on diagnostic devices

**Summary:** FDA has classified a simple IVD device for detecting secreted proteins from Bacillus species in human clinical samples as Class II (special controls). The agency determined Class II classification provides reasonable assurance of safety and effectiveness while reducing regulatory burden and improving patient access.

**Draft:**

FDA just classified a simple IVD for detecting secreted proteins from Bacillus spp. in human clinical samples as Class II (special controls). That's a lower bar than Class III, which matters a lot for how quickly something like this can actually get to a lab.

The framing in the Federal Register is pretty standard ("reasonable assurance of safety and effectiveness"), but the Bacillus detection angle is what caught my eye. Not something I see classified every day.

I'll be reading the special controls language when I get a chance. That's usually where the interesting requirements actually live.
