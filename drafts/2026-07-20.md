**Summary:** A rollout of 5,000 near point-of-care TB test systems across 13 countries is beginning, backed by $50 million in funding. The initiative aims to bring TB diagnostics closer to patients in high-burden settings.

5,000 near point-of-care TB test systems, 13 countries, $50M. That's a big logistics problem wrapped inside a diagnostics problem.

I don't know what the rollout plan looks like on the ground. I'm curious how they're handling that middle layer.

## 3. Cepheid POC Molecular Vaginitis Assay Improves Diagnostic Accuracy in Study

**Why:** Cepheid POC molecular assay clinical validation relevant to POC testing community

**Summary:** A study found that Cepheid's point-of-care molecular assay for vaginitis improved diagnostic accuracy compared to standard methods. The test runs at the point of care, meaning results are available during the clinical visit rather than days later.

**Draft:**

Vaginitis is one of those things that sounds simple but is genuinely hard to diagnose accurately without decent lab support. So a study showing a POC molecular assay improving diagnostic accuracy there is interesting to me, partly because molecular at the point of care still feels like a big deal in settings where you'd otherwise be waiting days for results (or just guessing).

I don't know the full study design, so I'm holding my enthusiasm loosely. But the direction makes sense. Faster and more accurate for something this common seems worth paying attention to.
